# Job search and applications

Find and compare openings, then help the person apply. Every posting you show comes from a named site or service. The application belongs to them: you fill it from facts they gave, and they decide what is sent.

## Kinds of request

- Role and place ("senior backend, Seattle or remote"): fill seniority, pay and dealbreakers from memory before searching.
- One company ("what is Stripe hiring for in design?"): their careers page, or filter by company.
- Recent only ("anything new for PMs since last week?"): the posted-date filter matters most.
- "Apply to this one" with a link: an application, not a search. Open that URL.

## Settle first

- Titles, location or remote, seniority, pay floor, work authorisation or visa limits, dealbreakers (no on-call, no relocation, no contracts). Default to full-time and say so.
- `Recall` résumé facts (titles, years, skills, employers), target titles, companies to avoid, pay floor. Never add a qualification they have not stated.
- "This week" is the posted-date filter set to the last seven days; do not judge from "2 days ago" labels.
- Use a jobs connector if one is listed.

## Search

1. Otherwise LinkedIn jobs or the employer's careers page, with keywords, location, date posted and remote filter in the URL. Playbook: `linkedin`. Looking applies to nothing and saves nothing.
2. Capture: title, company, location or remote, posted date, pay range and how to apply (Easy Apply or an outside site) when shown, URL.
3. "Remote only", "this week", "no agencies", "over $180k" are filters, not questions.
4. Drop postings that break a dealbreaker or fall under the pay floor, and say how many and why.

## Present

Three to five postings with those fields, plus plain notes on fit ("wants Go, which you haven't listed"; "senior title, range tops out at $150k"). For recent searches, newest first with real dates. No reposts or near-duplicates.

## Apply

1. Read the chosen posting fully and raise anything decisive: clearance, degree, office days, contract length, visa policy.
2. Fill from known facts; ask for the rest. Pay history and expectations, work authorisation, sponsorship and demographic questions are the person's to answer: leave them for them or hand over the screen. You never choose an answer to an optional demographic question. Long forms: one page at a time.
3. An outside hiring system asking for a fresh account: they create it (hand over).
4. Before submitting, or sending any connection request or recruiter message, read back the posting, the résumé or profile being attached, and every answer. Messages go into the question word for word, and only approved text is sent.
5. Easy Apply sends the résumé LinkedIn holds. Read its file name and date from the review step and include them; if they have not seen that file, ask which résumé to use.
6. If the review step shows an answer or field you did not read back, stop and show it.
7. Report the confirmation or "application sent" message, the job title and employer, and the next step the page describes.

## Watch for

- Reposts carry a fresh date on an old role; check the URL and the company's careers page before calling it new.
- Ghost postings: open for months, no pay, generic text. Voice doubt rather than shortlisting.
- Placeholder ranges ($50k–$300k) are not an offer; do not quote the midpoint.
- LinkedIn throttles fast browsing with checkpoints and human checks. Slow down, hand over once, and do not retry the wall in a loop.
