# Choosing where to eat

Pick restaurants like a friend who knows the city's food and knows this person. Good taste here means a specific point of view; cost and celebrity are not the measure: a neighbourhood counter can beat a famous dining room. A few places you stand behind beat a long list of acceptable ones.

## Kinds of request

- Party and night, no place ("where can four of us go Saturday?").
- An occasion and an area ("anniversary near the Mission").
- A comparison for a purpose ("Zuni or Nopa for a client dinner?").
- A dish and a place ("best ramen near me").

## Build the brief (privately)

- Hard limits: allergies, dietary rules, accessibility, stated dislikes, budget ceiling, distance, date and time, party, must-haves. Soft signals: mood, usual cuisines and dishes, room energy, adventurousness, usual spend, patience for travel and queues.
- Draw on, in this order: what they asked and said here; then `Recall` (a diet, places loved or refused, usual price); then, only where it would change the answer, a connected mail or calendar service via `connector_request`. Do not sweep their data.
- The explicit request wins. Specific, recent, confirmed preferences beat old or vague ones. Repeated behaviour beats one instance; a single clue is only a guess. Demographics tell you nothing about taste.
- Never infer an allergy or diet from order history. A companion's order or a group order says nothing about the person. Not having ordered something is not disliking it.
- Judge price on the whole meal (set menu, service charge, drinks), and infer a price band only from repeated behaviour or a stated preference.
- "Near me" with no known location: ask for the area. Otherwise decide and state your assumption.

## Steps

1. If one uncertain preference would change the answer, ask one good question with `AskUser`, not several general ones.
2. Gather candidates with `WebSearch`: respected local critics, editorial guides, cuisine specialists, local writers, and fresh discussion on forums such as r/finedining or the local subreddit. Keep more than you will show.
3. Critics and guides carry the taste judgement; threads add detail. Star ratings, popularity and virality only support. Lists copying lists are not separate votes. Check the critic you cite still holds that role.
4. Check each finalist with `WebFetch` against its own website and today's menu: what it cooks, its known dishes, whether it fits their diet, the format, roughly what it costs. Check opening hours for that day (a maps connector if listed, otherwise Google Maps in the browser), including any "temporarily closed".
5. Keep a place only if it meets every hard limit, fits this person specifically, shows real evidence of distinctive cooking, suits the group and room, works on price, distance and hours, and has not closed or changed chefs since the sources wrote about it. For an expensive or special meal, or a single pick, want two independent taste sources plus the restaurant's own site. A very new place with one strong specific source may stay, flagged.
6. Rank on fit and conviction. A star, list count, hard reservation, proximity or top rating is not a reason alone. Skip the obvious famous choice when a better-fitting one exists, and do not read "best" as "most expensive".
7. Imminent or hard-to-book meal: check availability without booking.
8. If checking weakens a pick, replace it.

## Presenting

- One firm pick when they asked for one or left it to you; three to five ranked only when real trade-offs remain. Each must earn its place.
- For each: name and area; cuisine, format, realistic price for the meal; why it suits them and this occasion; one or two dishes a source backs; the single drawback that could change the decision (shut on Mondays, fixed menu only, fully booked weeks out).
- Lead with the judgement and why it beats the obvious option. No research narration, accolade lists or scoring. Link the best current sources.
- Explain fit without exposing their data; mention a receipt or email only if it helps or they ask.
- When they choose, go to `restaurant-booking.md` or `food-ordering.md`, carrying the brief, and confirm anything still inferred before booking or paying.

## Watch for

- Suggesting a place from an old article or from memory without confirming it still runs as described.
- Recommending a place they refused, or a repeat when they wanted something new.
- Saving inferred taste; keep only what they stated ("I'm vegetarian", "no tasting menus").
- Making up opening hours, prices or dishes; name what you could not verify.
